# Tara at the Piano

## Why it looks like this

A dark ground makes the keyboard the brightest object on the screen, which is
where a four-year-old's eye should go. Brass for rules and labels, ivory for
keys and text.

## The hand colours are load-bearing

`rh` orange and `lh` green are the same pair Louise writes into Tara's book by
hand ("we have coloured right in orange and left in green to help with the hand
changes", 11 Jun 2026). They carry the same meaning everywhere: hand panel,
finger badge on a key, note head on the staff, chip in the strip. Never reuse
them for anything else.

## Three positions taken deliberately

- **No fail state.** A wrong key sounds the note and leaves the target lit.
- **No rhythm scoring.** She is learning which key, with which hand.
- **No guilt loop.** Nothing on screen ever gets sad, sick or disappointed.

## Both hands, always

The tune passes between the hands almost every note. A panel that appears only
when its hand is playing announces the swap too late, so both stay on screen:
the playing hand lit, the waiting hand dimmed with a dashed ring on the finger
it is about to need.
